ShayanSlides 1–3 · starts at 0:00

Slide 1 · Title 0:00

"Assalam-o-Alaikum everyone. Our project is called S-GH, the Greenhouse Microclimate Anomaly Detector.

A greenhouse is a glass house for growing plants. Microclimate means the weather inside it: heat, wetness, air and light. An anomaly means something going wrong.

So our system watches the weather inside a greenhouse and warns and fixes problems in seconds, even with no internet. It is all a computer simulation; we used no real hardware."

Slide 2 · The problem 1:00

"Look at these three pictures:

  • At night the heater breaks. It gets very cold and the plants freeze.
  • In the morning a roof window stays shut. It gets too hot and the plants burn.
  • The internet stops. Normally the data would be lost.

Our greenhouse has 5 parts, called zones. Each zone has 5 sensors that check it every 30 seconds. Sensor = a small device that measures something, like a thermometer."

Slide 3 · Our goals 2:00

"We set five simple goals:

  • Warn about a problem in less than 15 seconds.
  • Save one full day of data if the internet goes off.
  • Keep every message very small, under 200 bytes, like a short text message.
  • Fit on a small, cheap chip (a mini computer).
  • Give no more than one false alarm a day."

→ "Now Arqam will show our sensors and how the data travels."

2
ArqamSlides 4–6 · starts at about 3:00

Slide 4 · Our sensors 3:00

"Thank you, Shayan. These are our sensors. They are real products, but we simulate them in the computer:

  • SHT45: measures temperature and humidity. Humidity = how much water is in the air.
  • SCD40: measures CO₂. CO₂ = the gas plants breathe in to grow.
  • Soil probe: a stick in the soil that checks how wet the soil is.
  • BH1750: measures sunlight.
  • ESP32: a tiny, cheap computer chip (about $10) that reads all the sensors.

From a problem starting to a fan or heater switching on takes only about 6 seconds."

Slide 5 · How data travels 4:15

"Think of it like a post office. Each zone sends small letters with its readings.

  • The letters go to the edge gateway. Edge gateway = a small box inside the greenhouse that collects all the messages, like a local post office.
  • Then they go to the cloud. Cloud = big computers on the internet that store data.
  • We send them using MQTT. MQTT = a simple messaging system for small devices, like WhatsApp for machines.
  • QoS 1 means every message gets a "received" reply. If it is lost, it is sent again.

The padlock means the connection is locked and secure, so nobody can read or fake our data."

Slide 6 · How much data 5:15

"Each message is only 106 bytes. That's smaller than one WhatsApp text.

  • All 5 zones send 14,400 messages a day, but that is only about 1.5 MB, less than one photo.
  • If the internet goes off, each box saves its readings like a diary, enough for 24 hours.
  • When the internet comes back, it sends everything at once, with nothing lost."

→ "Now Hussain will explain the brain of our system, the AI."

3
HussainSlides 7–9 · starts at about 6:15

Slide 7 · The AI learns what is normal 6:15

"Thank you, Arqam. AI = a computer program that learns from data.

Our AI learns what a normal day looks like. In the chart, the green band is what the AI expects: warm in the day, cool at night. The black line is the real temperature.

When the heater breaks at night, the black line falls out of the green band. The AI notices straight away and gives an alert, the red dot."

Slide 8 · Two layers of AI 7:15

"Our AI works like two security guards inside the greenhouse:

  • Guard 1, simple rules. For example: "Heater is ON but it is getting colder? Something is wrong!" This works from day one.
  • Guard 2, the autoencoder. Autoencoder = a small AI that learns what normal readings look like and notices when something is different. It is tiny, only 14 KB.
  • The cloud keeps all old data and teaches the AI again every week, so it keeps getting better."

Slide 9 · Broken sensor or real problem? 8:15

"Sometimes the sensor itself breaks.

  • Left picture: the humidity sensor shows 0%. That is impossible, so the sensor is broken. We don't ring the alarm; we just ask someone to repair it.
  • Right picture: real frost. The temperature drops and the humidity goes up at the same time. Two signs together prove it's real, so the alarm rings and the heater turns on.

Our rule: we never delete strange data; we only mark it, so real problems are never missed."

→ "Now Ali will show the full system and our results."

4
AliSlides 10–12 · starts at about 9:15

Slide 10 · The full system 9:15

"Thank you, Hussain. Read this picture from left to right.

  • Green area, inside the greenhouse: sensors measure, then bad readings are cleaned out, then the AI decides, then fans or heaters switch on in seconds.
  • Blue area, the cloud: stores all the data, shows charts on a dashboard and sends alerts to the farmer's phone. Dashboard = a screen with live charts.

The most important idea: decisions are made inside the greenhouse. So even with no internet, the plants stay safe."

Slide 11 · Results 10:15

"We built a working simulation. Simulation = a pretend greenhouse made in the computer that behaves like a real one.

In the picture, the heater in Zone 3 broke at night. Our system noticed and turned on the backup heater by itself in about 6 seconds.

  • 0 false alarms in 6 test days.
  • Every real problem was caught: heater, roof window and water pump.
  • Broken sensors were ignored safely.
  • No data was lost when the internet went off."

Slide 12 · Summary 11:15

"So our system watches, cleans, decides and fixes, all in seconds, even without internet. The cloud helps it keep learning.

The road at the bottom shows our next steps, from simulation to a real greenhouse.

In short, our greenhouse takes care of itself, so the farmer can sleep peacefully."

"Thank you! Any questions?"

Easy wordsAll the technical words, explained simply
Greenhouse
A glass house where plants are grown.
Microclimate
The weather inside the greenhouse: heat, wetness, air and light.
Anomaly
Something unusual or going wrong.
AIoT
AI + IoT: smart devices that measure things and a computer brain that understands them.
IoT
Internet of Things: small devices connected to the internet.
Sensor
A small device that measures something, like a thermometer.
Zone
One part (room) of the greenhouse. We have 5.
Humidity (RH)
How much water is in the air.
CO₂
The gas plants breathe in to make food.
SHT45
Sensor for temperature and humidity.
SCD40
Sensor for CO₂.
BH1750
Sensor for sunlight.
Soil probe
A stick in the soil that checks how wet the soil is.
ESP32
A tiny, cheap computer chip that reads the sensors.
Edge
Inside the greenhouse, close to the plants (not on the internet).
Edge gateway
A small box in the greenhouse that collects all messages, like a local post office.
Cloud
Big computers on the internet that store data.
MQTT
A simple messaging system for small devices, like WhatsApp for machines.
QoS 1
Every message gets a "received" reply; if lost, it is sent again.
TLS / padlock
A locked, secret connection so nobody can read or fake the data.
Byte / KB / MB
Sizes of data. 1 KB ≈ 1,000 bytes, 1 MB ≈ 1,000 KB. A photo is a few MB.
Buffer
Saved data kept on the device, like a diary, until the internet is back.
Autoencoder
A small AI that learns what normal looks like and notices when something is different.
False alarm
An alarm when nothing is actually wrong.
Dashboard
A screen with live charts and numbers.
Simulation
A pretend greenhouse made in the computer that behaves like a real one.
