Simulation script
How the live demo works

Easy language · about 5 minutes · presented by Ali (R4) · open the website and scroll to "Break it. Watch it react."

How to read this script
  • Green-blue box = what to click or do.
  • Normal text = what to say.
  • Yellow box = what the audience should look at on screen.

Part 1 · What is this simulation? ~30 s

"This is our live simulation: a pretend greenhouse running inside the web browser. We have no real hardware, so the computer acts like a real greenhouse.

It uses simple physics. The sun heats the glass, heat escapes at night, the heater adds warmth, the roof windows let hot air out, and the pump waters the soil. So when we break something, the greenhouse reacts the way a real one would.

Time runs fast here: every 1.5 seconds on screen is 6 minutes in the greenhouse. A full day passes in about 6 minutes."

Part 2 · A tour of the screen ~1.5 min

The buttons on top

"These buttons are our scenarios. Each one breaks something on purpose so we can see how the system reacts. They are grouped as Baseline (normal), True anomalies (real problems), Sensor faults (broken sensors) and Network (internet off).

On the right is the clock (time and day in the greenhouse), the speed (1× or 3×), pause, and reset."

The greenhouse picture

"This is our greenhouse with 5 zones (rooms): Tomato, Basil, Lettuce, Pepper and Mint. Each zone shows its temperature in big numbers, with humidity and CO₂ below.

  • The small black box with a light is the sensor box. Its light is green when OK, amber for a sensor fault and red for an alert.
  • The roof windows (vents) open when it gets warm and close when it's cold.
  • The heater pipes glow orange when the heater is on, the fans spin when cooling, and water drops fall when the pump waters the soil.
  • The sky changes with time: sun in the day, moon and stars at night.
  • The little glowing dots are data messages flying to the edge gateway (the post-office box on the pole) and then up to the cloud."
Look: the small chips at the top-left show the outside temperature, how strong the sun is, and how cloudy it is.

The zone cards (below the greenhouse)

"Each card is one zone. It shows the temperature, a small line of recent history, humidity, soil wetness and CO₂, and a status tag: OK, FAULT or ALERT. Clicking a card or a zone focuses it, so all the panels below show that zone."

The chart and the AI panel

  • Chart: the last 8 hours of the chosen zone. The green line is temperature, the light green band is what the AI expects, the teal line is humidity, and the red area at the bottom is the danger score. Night hours are shaded dark.
  • Dual-layer AI rings: the inner ring is Layer 1 (rules), the outer ring is Layer 2 (the autoencoder), and the number in the middle is the danger score. If it reaches 0.60, we raise an alert.
  • Actual vs expected: for each sensor, the dot is the real value and the green mark is what the AI expected. A red dot means "this one looks wrong"."

The bottom panels

  • Edge pipeline: the steps every reading goes through: Sensors → Cleaning (Stage 5) → Layer 1 rules → Layer 2 AI → Decision → Action and sending. Each box lights up as data passes.
  • MQTT inspector: the actual message being sent: zone, temperature, humidity, soil, CO₂, light. It's only about 106 bytes, and QoS 1 means it must be confirmed as received.
  • Ring buffer: the 24-hour backup memory, drawn as a ring of 24 hours. It fills up only when the internet is off.
  • Event log: a diary of everything that happened, with times."

The AI training box (when the page first opens)

"When the page opens, you briefly see 'Training autoencoder' with a line going down. The AI is learning right here in the browser from 3 days of normal greenhouse data. Until it finishes, only the simple rules protect the plants. We call this the cold start."

Part 3 · Breaking things on purpose ~3 min

1 · Normal day key 1

Click "Normal day" (or press 1).

"Everything is fine. All zones are green and OK. The temperature follows a normal day: warm in the afternoon, cool at night. The danger score stays near zero, so there are no false alarms."

2 · Heater failure at night key 2 · Zone 3 Lettuce

Click "Heater failure". The clock jumps to night automatically.

"It's night and the heater in Zone 3 breaks. Watch the temperature fall. Frost starts to form on the glass and the plants turn bluish.

Within a few seconds the zone turns red with an ALERT and a red banner explains it in plain words: 'Zone 3 is colder than expected while the heater is ON.'

Then the system fixes it by itself: it closes the vents and switches on the backup heater, and you can see the pipes glow orange. The temperature comes back up and the alert clears."

Look: the red outline on Zone 3, the score ring going red, and the event log line "ALERT … backup heater ON".

3 · Roof window (vent) jammed key 3 · Zone 4 Pepper

Click "Vent jammed". The clock jumps to a sunny morning.

"On a sunny morning the greenhouse heats up, so the roof window should open to let hot air out. But in Zone 4 the window is stuck shut.

The system notices that the window was told to open but didn't move, and the zone keeps getting hotter. It raises an alert, then turns on the fans and closes a shade screen to cool the plants."

Look: the fans start spinning in Zone 4 and the alert says the vents were commanded open but aren't moving.

4 · Water pump failure key 4 · Zone 5 Mint

Click "Pump failure". The clock jumps to just before watering time.

"Plants are watered automatically at set times. Here the pump in Zone 5 breaks, so at watering time no water comes and the soil doesn't get wetter.

A rule notices that watering was supposed to happen, but the soil stayed dry. It raises an alert and starts a backup pump. Now you can see water drops falling and the soil getting wet again."

5 · Broken humidity sensor (stuck at 0%) key 5 · Zone 2 Basil

Click "RH stuck 0 %".

"Now the sensor itself is broken: Zone 2's humidity sensor suddenly says 0%. That's impossible inside a greenhouse.

Our cleaning step (Stage 5) catches it. The zone shows an amber SENSOR FAULT, but there is no alarm, because the plants are fine. It only asks for a repair.

Look at the AI panel: the raw, uncleaned score would be 1.00, a false alarm, but after cleaning the real score stays low. That's why cleaning matters."

Look: in the message inspector the bad value is highlighted in yellow, and the chart shows the raw humidity line dropping while the cleaned line stays normal.

6 · One crazy reading (−40 °C glitch) key 6 · Zone 1 Tomato

Click "Glitch −40 °C".

"Sometimes a sensor gives one crazy reading, like −40 °C, because of a loose wire. Stage 5 sees it is impossible, ignores that one reading, and the next normal reading continues. Again, no false alarm."

7 · Internet goes off, then comes back key 7

Click "Gateway offline". Wait a few seconds, then click "Reconnect gateway".

"Now the internet goes off. The line to the cloud turns red with an X. But the greenhouse keeps working: it still checks the plants and still fixes problems by itself.

Every sensor box saves its readings in its 24-hour backup memory. You can see the ring buffer filling up and the stack of bars next to the gateway growing.

When I reconnect, all the saved data is sent in one quick burst, the buffer empties, and the log says '0 gaps, 0 duplicates', so nothing was lost or doubled."

Tip: press 2 while offline to show that the heater alert still works with no internet.

Back to normal key 1

Click "Normal day" to clear everything.

"All problems are cleared and the greenhouse is back to a normal day.

So in our simulation we tested 3 real problems (heater, roof window, pump), 2 broken-sensor cases, and internet loss. The system caught every real problem in seconds, fixed it by itself, ignored broken sensors without false alarms, and lost no data. Thank you!"
